# STW Divide And Conquer

**Author:** Ramon.Talavera@gmail.com / ScienceTechWorks@gmail.com

## General principles

**IMPORTANT:** The enemy does not know your position by default. An enemy can locate you only when a unit sees you - including occupants of vehicles and helicopters - or when intelligence reports your position. If detected, move immediately. Camouflage reduces the enemy's ability to detect and track a unit, but it does not prevent direct observation.

## Tactical map display

The tactical map has three display modes: no hexagonal grid, a semitransparent grid, and an opaque grid. Faction colours identify zone ownership, while yellow marks the front-line zone. Enemy surveillance information is stronger in its own zones, and the chance of inferring a player's position increases in enemy territory and especially at the front. Border zones may be mined, particularly military bases and strategic points.

## Dynamic campaign

Faction-coloured icons show ownership. Friendly troops also fight, occupy positions, and change the front over time, so the map is dynamic. A mission is placed in a zone for a limited time. When the timer expires, success is calculated from time held and faction morale. Morale is affected by political, economic, social, and temporal conditions. If a zone is not captured before the deadline, it is much more likely to change sides. Occupation speed varies; Special Forces can take a position within a few hours, so their presence requires a rapid response.

## Mission types

### Strongholds

The defending faction fortifies buildings, places marksmen, runs infantry and vehicle patrols, and establishes roadblocks. Occupied buildings provide resistance and must be cleared on site. The attacker must take the stronghold before the timer expires. The victory percentage is the percentage of forces that must be eliminated, not the total number of troops in the zone. Enemies may remain after victory, so extraction can still be dangerous.

### Close Quarters Battle - CQB

Friendly and enemy troops occupy streets and buildings. Hold the zone for the indicated occupation time and inflict the required number of enemy casualties. Even when both conditions are met, enemy troops may remain; plan the extraction with that threat in mind.

### Special Forces

The objective is to eliminate every member of the group. Special Forces are highly equipped and can deploy a drone. If the drone detects the players, the unit knows their position and begins harassment. One member controls the drone; if the operator is killed, the drone loses control and crashes. Drones cannot enter forests or buildings. If a drone is detected, flee and seek cover. The same advice applies to the air-superiority drone.

### Battle Zones

Battle Zones attract large numbers of friendly and enemy troops. Once established, they have a high force presence. The objective is to inflict enough enemy casualties.

## Aerial surveillance and allied drone reconnaissance

The enemy aerial-surveillance drone searches the sky for enemy troops. Detection triggers a surveillance warning and increases the risk of mortar fire, close air support (CAS), troop insertions, and enemy reinforcements.

An allied drone scan is different: it is requested over a selected zone. The result shows contacts and tactical markers detected inside that area, giving the commander information for movement, mission planning, or support requests.

## Command wheel

### Deployment

Use the wheel to request parachute deployment of vehicles and crates with varied contents.

### Reconnaissance

Request a drone scan over a selected zone to obtain tactical information before moving or calling support.

### Transport and insertion

Request helicopter transport from pickup point A to destination B. Transport can support rope insertion where available, and the wheel can also request helicopter insertion troops.

### Combat support

Request CAS and ground combat support, including APCs, jeeps, and tanks. Mortar support includes explosive rounds, smoke rounds, and illumination flares.
